# Neuroscience and learning principles

These principles are design constraints, not decoration.

## Retrieval beats rereading

The app should repeatedly ask the learner to explain, recall, and restructure. Editing the map should be treated as learning, not merely organization.

## Reduce working-memory load

Complex material should be chunked into small blocks. A learner should be able to focus on one block, one link, or one sub-map at a time.

## Use dual coding

Pair words with spatial layout, color, shape, and relationship type. Visual encoding should add meaning, not clutter.

## Use elaboration

Relationship lines should force the learner to say why two ideas connect: causes, funds, controls, costs, evidence, contrast, loop, or exit.

## Support ADHD attention regulation

- low visual clutter by default;
- collapsed help panels;
- optional focus mode;
- visible progress but not constant distraction;
- quick recenter/recovery controls;
- forgiving interaction model.

## Support dyslexia accessibility

- Comic Sans or similar dyslexia-friendly fallback stack;
- generous line-height and spacing;
- plain language;
- read-aloud and sentence highlighting;
- avoid dense paragraphs inside blocks;
- avoid tiny contrast-poor text.

## Make restructuring easy

Moving blocks and changing relationships should be frictionless because restructuring strengthens consolidation. The app should make it safe to experiment, reset, export, and recover.
